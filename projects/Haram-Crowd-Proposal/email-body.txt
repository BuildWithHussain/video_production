Subject: Suggestion: a metro-style entry token for the Mataf, so first-time Umrah pilgrims can complete Istilam and pray at the Multazam


Assalamu alaikum wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuh,

Respected Sir/Madam,

First, thank you for the work the Ministry has already done. Visas, permits and entry to Masjid al-Haram are far more organised today than they were a few years ago. The Mataf now serves more than 100,000 pilgrims an hour across the ground floor, the upper floors and the roof, and the permit system in Madinah has brought order to the Rawdah. These show that the Kingdom is willing to fix crowding with fair systems. We write only to add one small suggestion in the same spirit.

The problem we faced

My father performed Umrah recently, along with many people from our community and from around the world. Almost none of them could reach Al-Hajar Al-Aswad for Istilam, or stand at the Multazam and pray. Many had saved for years for one journey, and came back without it. The reason is that the ground floor of the Mataf is very crowded, and much of that crowd is not first-time Umrah pilgrims. People are told to perform their first Umrah on the ground floor and later Tawaf from the upper floors. But in practice, anyone wearing Ihram can walk onto the ground floor, and many who have already finished their Umrah, some of them in normal clothes, keep performing Tawaf there. So the pilgrim on his one and only journey is pushed away from the very place he came for. The elderly and the women suffer the most.

Our suggestion: a metro-style token for the ground floor

We suggest a physical token, not a code on a phone. A code on a screen can be photographed, shared and used again. A token is handed over once, taken by the gate, and it is finished. The pilgrim would get his token along with his Umrah visa when he arrives, at the same counter where his documents are processed. That token lets him enter the ground floor of the Mataf one time. He walks to the gate, drops the token in like a metro turnstile, and goes in. No queue at a counter, no phone, no app, and nothing to explain in a language he may not speak.

Two simple rules do the rest. First, a token is given only to a pilgrim who has not already been given one in that Hijri year. So everybody gets a turn, and nobody can take the ground floor again and again in one season. Second, the token has no fixed time. The pilgrim keeps it and uses it on the day he finds the Mataf lighter, or on his second Umrah instead of his first, whichever suits him. Everyone else, meaning people on their second or third Umrah and people doing voluntary Tawaf, would use the upper floors, which are already comfortable and have plenty of space. This is the part that Ihram alone can never control today. A token at the gate settles it, with no argument between the pilgrim and the staff.

Inside the Mataf: a spiral of lanes, one for women and one for men

This is the heart of our suggestion. Once the pilgrim enters, low dividers guide him into a lane, and the lanes are arranged as a spiral. He begins his first circuit on the outermost ring, farthest from the Kaaba. With each circuit the lane brings him one ring closer. By the sixth and seventh circuit he is walking right beside the Kaaba, so he finishes his Tawaf standing at the place where he needs to be, next to the Multazam and Al-Hajar Al-Aswad, without ever having to cut across the crowd. Today a pilgrim has to fight his way inward from the outer ring, and this is where most of the pushing and the danger comes from. A spiral removes that completely, because moving inward is no longer something he has to do for himself. It is simply where the lane takes him.

There would be two such spirals running side by side, one for the ladies and one for the gents. Neither can push into the other, because a divider is between them the whole way. Both spirals end at the same place, so a husband and wife, or a family that travelled together, arrive at the end at the same time. They can then pray their two rakaat, perform Istilam, and walk out together toward Bab as-Safa for their Sa'y. Families do not have to worry about being separated, which is one of the biggest fears people carry into the Mataf today.

With a lighter crowd and clear lanes, the last part of the rite can then be handled in small groups, in the same way as the Rawdah in Madinah. A small batch of pilgrims is let through to the Multazam and the Hijr Ismail to pray their two rakaat, ladies and gents in turns, each group given a short fixed time of a minute or two. They are then guided forward for Istilam and out through a separate exit lane. Every pilgrim in that batch has already finished his circuits, so everyone moves in one direction and the Tawaf itself never has to stop. People leave through the lane as they finish, just as they do today, but in an orderly line instead of a struggle. And if a pilgrim has to step out to renew his wudu, the same metro idea works again. He hands in a card on the way out and takes it back on the way in, so he does not have to start all over again.

The dividers would not be fixed walls. They can be opened or moved aside. So at the times of the five daily prayers, or at any other time the scholars advise, a section can simply be opened up and the area used as the scholars direct, for the ladies, for the gents, or for everyone together. The lanes serve the pilgrims; they would never stand in the way of the prayer.

We know that the Ministry and the General Presidency understand the Haram far better than we ever could, and that any idea like this would need your study and the guidance of the scholars for matters such as prayer times. We send this only as what one family saw on their journey. We have also thought through the practical side in detail, including how the lanes and barricades would be built and what they would be made of, how many people each batch should hold, how the tokens would be collected and returned, and what to do in the difficult cases. We have kept all of that out of this letter to keep it short.

We would be grateful if you could review this proposal and let us know what you think. If the idea is useful to you, we are ready to explain the full plan, in writing or in person, and to answer any questions you may have.

May Allah reward the Kingdom and everyone serving the guests of the Rahman.

With respect and gratitude,
